The Founder

Jack Wattley's Dream

The discus is a beautiful flat round fish found throughout the Amazon basin of South America. For many years it was one of the most difficult species to breed.

Nearly 60 years ago, Jack Wattley had a dream. He traveled to the Amazon and personally collected wild-caught discus from different parts of the basin, bringing them back to Florida. His goal was to raise them in captivity and unlock their full potential through selective breeding.

He began cross-breeding discus and developing entirely new color morphs. To this day, Jack is recognized as the creator of the blue turquoise discus — better known as the Wattley Turquoise — among many other landmark strains.

The Partnership

How Gabriel Posada Joined the Legacy

In 1991, Gabriel Posada met Jack Wattley and purchased six Red Panda discus from him. Within two years, those six fish became three breeding pairs.

Two years after the purchase, Jack suffered a setback — a raccoon got into his fish room and killed his last breeding pair of Red Pandas. He kept records of every fish he had sold and reached out to former customers, hoping to recover the strain.

One of the only local customers who had bought this strain was Gabe Posada. When Jack contacted him, he received the surprise of his life: Gabe had not only kept the fish — he had bred them successfully and already raised 15 breeding pairs from the offspring.

It was then that Wattley saw the opportunity. He invited Gabe into his breeding program, and exactly 23 years ago the partnership between Gabriel Posada and Jack Wattley was forged. Jack quickly discovered Gabe's deep passion for discus and tested his knowledge constantly.

Drawing on his background in engineering, Gabe created his own water filtration systems and custom tools for the hatchery. Today, Posada continues to follow and respect the Wattley method — enriched by his own contributions over nearly three decades.

— Gabriel Posada, Operations Manager, Jack Wattley Discus
